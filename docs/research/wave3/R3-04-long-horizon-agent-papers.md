## 1. MAST

PAPER: "Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail?", arXiv 2503.13657. v1 (March 2025, 151 traces), v2 (200+ traces), v3 26 Oct 2025 (1642 traces), NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks track (stated on page 1 of v3). Earlier ICLR 2025 workshop version exists (cited that way by Terminal-Bench).

WHAT EARLIER DOCS SAID: step repetition 15.7%, unaware of termination 12.4%, verification ~23.5% (W2-05). An audit read the repo figure as 11.5 / 6.54 / premature termination 8.64 / verification 31.41, and BUILD_PLAN.md line 150 rejected W2-05 on that basis.

WHAT THE PAPER SAYS:
- v3, Figure 1 and Section 4 text: "step repetitions (FM-1.3, 15.7%), context loss (FM-1.4, 2.80%), or not recognizing task completion (FM-1.5, 12.4%)". Full v3 table (FM, % of 1642 traces): 1.1 disobey task spec 11.8; 1.2 disobey role 1.50; 1.3 step repetition 15.7; 1.4 loss of history 2.80; 1.5 unaware of termination 12.4 (category FC1 44.2); 2.1 conversation reset 2.20; 2.2 fail to ask clarification 6.80; 2.3 task derailment 7.40; 2.4 info withholding 0.80; 2.5 ignored other agent 1.90; 2.6 reasoning-action mismatch 13.2 (FC2 32.3); 3.1 premature termination 6.20; 3.2 no or incomplete verification 8.20; 3.3 incorrect verification 9.10 (FC3 23.5). The FC sums check: 6.2 + 8.2 + 9.1 = 23.5.
- So "verification ~23.5%" is the category total (FC3), not FM 3.2. The percentages are prevalence of each mode across traces (multi-label), annotated by an o1 LLM judge (kappa 0.77 vs humans with few-shot; Table 2: accuracy 0.94, kappa 0.77).
- The 11.5 / 6.54 / 8.64 / 9.16 / 13.61, categories 37.17 / 31.41 / 31.41 numbers also appear in v3 inside Figure 2 (a workflow schematic, no trace count in the caption), and they are in v1 Figure 1 where the caption says "analysis of 151 traces". They are the early Grounded-Theory-stage numbers and are superseded. Note 31.41 appears for both FC2 and FC3 in that figure (coincidence of sums).
- Traces / systems / models (v3 Table 1): 7 MAS (ChatDev, MetaGPT, HyperAgent, AppWorld, AG2/MathChat, Magentic-One, OpenManus); models GPT-4o, GPT-4, Claude-3.7-Sonnet, GPT-4o-mini, Qwen2.5-Coder-32B, CodeLlama-7b; tasks ProgramDev(-v2), SWE-bench Lite, AppWorld Test-C, GSM-Plus, OlympiadBench, MMLU, GAIA. 1642 traces, mostly LLM-annotated (human-annotated subset about 210). These are 2024-25 era multi-agent frameworks, not Claude Code-class single-agent CLIs.
- Per-system profile differs: Section 5.1 says AppWorld "frequently suffers from premature terminations (FM-3.1)", OpenManus tends to step repetition, HyperAgent dominant FM-1.3 and FM-3.3 (Figure 4, 30 traces per system). "No one-size-fits-all".
- Appendix J.1, Table 7 (ChatDev/MetaGPT, n about 10-20 per cell): 1.5 and 2.4 appear almost only in failed runs; 3.2 and 3.3 appear often even in successful runs ("non-fatal").

VERDICT: 15.7 and 12.4 CONFIRMED (v3). 23.5 CONFIRMED only as the FC3 category total, WRONG if used as FM 3.2 (8.2%). 11.5 / 6.54 / 8.64 / 31.41 WRONG for the current paper (they are v1 / early-stage). Consequence: in v3, premature termination (6.2%) is LESS common than unaware of termination (12.4%), so W2-05's "premature done is rarer than non-termination" is supported by v3; the BUILD_PLAN rejection (which used v1 numbers 8.64 > 6.54) should be reversed or reworded. Scope caveat: mix depends on system (AppWorld is premature-termination heavy), and these are multi-agent framework traces from 2024-25.

Corroboration from 2026 frontier agents: Terminal-Bench (2601.11868, ICLR 2026) built its trajectory failure taxonomy from MAST (Execution / Coherence / Verification) and runs it on Opus 4.5, GPT-5.2, Qwen3-Coder-480B with Terminus 2 (Figure 8; the paper says execution errors dominate for frontier closed models; bar values not extractable from text). SWE-Marathon Table 4 (526 failures): timeout 31.4% (includes reasoning loops, stuck states), premature termination 7.6%, poor self-verification 4.0%, reward hacking 15.4%, implementation failure 41.6%.

## 2. METR time horizons

PAPER: "Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks" (title in v4; v1 title "Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Tasks"), arXiv 2503.14499 v4, 10 Jul 2026. Preprint (no venue on first page). 170 tasks (HCAST, RE-Bench, 66 SWAA), 12 frontier models 2019-2025, human baselines.

WHAT EARLIER DOCS SAID: 80% horizon about 4-5x shorter; doubling about 7 months; "decompose goals over roughly 1-2 hours of human-equivalent work".

WHAT THE PAPER SAYS:
- Abstract: "o3 have a 50% time horizon of around 110 minutes... doubled approximately every seven months since 2019, though the trend may have accelerated since 2024."
- Section 3.2: "time horizon has doubled every 207 days with a 95% bootstrapped confidence interval 166-240 days".
- Section 3.2.1: "The doubling time in 80% time horizon (204 days) is similar to... 207 days... However, models' 80% time horizons are 4-6x shorter". Introduction says "roughly 5x shorter" (Figure 17). Claude 3.7 Sonnet 80% horizon was about 0.25x its 50% horizon (Ord, below).
- Appendix F.1: 2023-2025 doubling about 6 months (172 days on non-SWAA tasks); 2024-only models about 3 months, "any extrapolation... would not be robust". SWE-bench Verified replication: doubling about 70 days (Section 4.1 area).
- Section 3.3 / Table 2 (31 GPT-4 1106 and 32 o1 failed runs): "Repeating failed actions" 12/31 GPT-4 vs 2/32 o1; premature task abandonment 8/31 vs 16/32. Evidence that loops are a model-generation-dependent failure and premature stop is the larger failure for stronger models.
- Success versus human time (Section 2): exponential decline, R2 about 0.80; agents do worse on "messier" tasks (F.2). Appendix H.3: success plateaus well below the maximum token budget (more tokens do not help past a point).
- 2026 frontier models: NOT-IN-PAPER (v4 stops at o3 / Claude 3.7). Via WebSearch [SNIPPET] only, metr.org UNREACHABLE: METR Time Horizon 1.1 (Jan 2026) hybrid doubling about 196 days, post-2023 about 131 days; Opus 4.5 50% about 4h49m, 80% about 27 min; Frontier Risk Report (May 2026) public-frontier 50% about 12 h (range 5-61 h) and 80% about 1.5 h (range 50 min to 2 h 40 min); METR note (Mar 2026) says alternative fits could cut 50% horizons up to 35% and raise 80% horizons up to 100%. Treat as unverified.
- Independent arXiv replications (read): BRIDGE (2602.07267 v2, Jul 2026) reproduces "50% solvable task horizon doubling approximately every 6 months", 80% doubling also about 6 months, and "From GPT-3.5... to Claude 4.5 Opus, the 80% task-length horizon... approximately under 1 hour of estimated human effort". Ord, "Is there a half-life for the success rates of AI agents?" (2505.05115, arXiv note, not peer reviewed): constant-hazard model predicts 80% horizon = ln(0.8)/ln(0.5) = 0.322 of the 50% horizon (about 3.1x shorter); METR's 0.25 is close.

VERDICT: ratio CONFIRMED-WITH-SCOPE-CAVEAT (paper says 4-6x; "roughly 5x"; theory says about 3.1x; fit noise is large). Doubling 207 days (about 7 months) CONFIRMED, with faster recent fits (3-6 months; later METR numbers [SNIPPET]). 2026 horizons NOT-IN-PAPER.
Does horizon data support "decompose over about 1-2 h"? Partly. The horizon is the human-expert duration of tasks an agent completes at a given success rate, on METR's suite (well-specified, auto-scored). For a factory that wants about 80%+ first-attempt success per leaf, the best 2026 80% figures I could find are about 0.5-1.5 h ([SNIPPET] METR; BRIDGE "under 1 hour" for Opus 4.5), so leaves of about 30-60 min human-equivalent with retry and verification are consistent, and 1-2 h is the top of the 80% range, not a safe default. 50% horizons are several hours, so 1-2 h leaves would likely succeed more often than not. Messiness lowers performance (F.2), and none of this measures the factory's own task mix. Keep as a guess and calibrate.

## 3. Huang et al.

PAPER: "Large Language Models Cannot Self-Correct Reasoning Yet", arXiv 2310.01798 v2 (14 Mar 2024), ICLR 2024 (stated in file).

WHAT EARLIER DOCS SAID: self-correction without external feedback degrades accuracy.

WHAT THE PAPER SAYS: Abstract: "LLMs struggle to self-correct their responses without external feedback, and at times, their performance even degrades after self-correction." Section 3.2: "after self-correction, the accuracies of all models drop across all benchmarks." Table 3 (intrinsic, GPT-3.5 / GPT-4): GSM8K 75.9 to 75.1 (round 1) to 74.7 (round 2) / 95.5 to 91.5 to 89.0; CommonSenseQA 75.8 to 38.1 to 41.8 / 82.0 to 79.5 to 80.0; HotpotQA 26.0 to 25.0 to 25.0 / 49.0 to 49.0 to 43.0. Table 4: GPT-4-Turbo GSM8K 91.5 to 88.0 to 90.0, CSQA 84.0 to 81.5 to 83.0; Llama-2-70B-chat GSM8K 62.0 to 43.5 to 36.5, CSQA 64.0 to 37.5 to 36.5. Tables 5-6: other feedback prompts also decrease performance. Table 2 (oracle labels, which the paper argues are an unfair setup): GPT-3.5 GSM8K 75.9 to 84.3, GPT-4 95.5 to 97.5. Section 4: multi-agent debate does not beat self-consistency at equal responses (Table 7: debate round 2 83.0 at 9 responses vs self-consistency 88.2). Mechanism: the model changes right answers to wrong ones (e.g. GPT-3.5 keeps its GSM8K answer 74.7% of the time).

VERDICT: CONFIRMED-WITH-SCOPE-CAVEAT. Scope: reasoning QA (GSM8K, CommonSenseQA, HotpotQA), 2023-era models (GPT-3.5-Turbo, GPT-4, GPT-4-Turbo, Llama-2-70B), up to 2 rounds, prompted self-critique, 100-200 sample subsets for some. Not coding agents with test feedback; the paper itself says external feedback (code executors, etc.) can help. Do not cite it as evidence about 2026 agents; cite it for "no LLM-only self-grading", which matches the plan's rule.

## 4. OpenHands

PAPER: "OpenHands: An Open Platform for AI Software Developers as Generalist Agents", arXiv 2407.16741, ICLR 2025 (stated in file). Table: CodeActAgent v1.8 SWE-bench Lite 26.0% with claude-3-5-sonnet-20240620 ($1.10 avg), 22.0% gpt-4o ($1.72).

WHAT EARLIER DOCS SAID: defaults 4 identical action+observation, 3 same-error, 3 A/B ping-pong cycles, monologue 3 (BUILD_PLAN line 86: nudge at 3, kill at 4th same-action error).

WHAT THE PAPER SAYS: grep for stuck / loop / repeat / monologue / identical / ping finds nothing relevant. NOT-IN-PAPER. It does not describe a stuck detector or give evidence for its thresholds.

WHAT THE CODE SAYS (tool defaults, not evidence):
- Current SDK, github OpenHands/software-agent-sdk `openhands-sdk/openhands/sdk/conversation/types.py` class `StuckDetectionThresholds`: `action_observation=4`, `action_error=3`, `monologue=3`, `alternating_pattern=6`. Detector in `stuck_detector.py` (read from raw.githubusercontent.com, main, retrieved 2026-10-07). Scans only events since the last user message, window of 20 events.
- Semantics: (1) same action AND same observation 4 times in a row. (2) same action ending in an error, at threshold 3 the harness emits a nudge once ("Repeating the exact same call again will not work... try a different approach"), and `is_stuck` fires only when the streak exceeds the threshold, i.e. on the 4th. (3) monologue: 3 consecutive agent messages without observation between them. (4) alternating pattern: threshold 6 means 6 actions and 6 observations with even entries equal and odd entries equal, which is 3 A/B cycles. (5) repeated context-window/condensation errors (10 events). Thresholds are configurable in the SDK.
- Older V0 code (All-Hands-AI/OpenHands tag 0.30.0, `openhands/controller/stuck.py`): 4 identical action-observation pairs; action-error stuck at 3 (no nudge); monologue 3; A/B pattern on last 6 steps; context-window loop at 10. So "nudge at 3, kill at 4" is the SDK's behavior, not V0's.
- [SNIPPET] SDK issue #762 reports thresholds were hardcoded and that a legitimate "poll every 5 minutes" task trips the 4-repeat rule (false positive). The SDK file I read does now take configurable thresholds.

VERDICT: The five defaults CONFIRMED as OpenHands tool defaults; the "3 A/B cycles" wording is correct (6 actions). They are engineering heuristics with no published calibration. Independent evidence that identical-call runs matter: SWE-Marathon (section 9) and METR Table 2.

## 6. Tree search, backtracking, resampling

SWE-Search: "SWE-Search: Enhancing Software Agents with Monte Carlo Tree Search and Iterative Refinement", arXiv 2410.20285 v6 (2 Apr 2025), ICLR 2025. Table 1 (SWE-bench Lite resolve %, Moatless-Adapted baseline to SWE-Search): GPT-4o 25.7 to 31.0 (+17% rel), GPT-4o-mini 13.0 to 17.0 (+24), Qwen-2.5-72B 18.0 to 24.7 (+27), DeepSeek-V2.5 16.3 to 21.0 (+22), Llama-3.1-70B 13.6 to 17.7 (+23); mean +23% relative (about +4-7 points absolute). Cost (Table 3): GPT-4o $40.86 to $576.00 (about 14x), GPT-4o-mini $9.90 to $52.34. Compute-matched (Table 4): SWE-Search pass@5 (5 answers from 1 run) vs baseline pass@5 over 5 runs: GPT-4o-mini 22.3 vs 17.0, Qwen 25.7 vs 22.3, DeepSeek 23.3 vs 22.0, Llama 22.3 vs 21.7, so the advantage over compute-matched resampling is small (0.6-5.3 points). Max 100 iterations, depth 20. 2024-era models, 300 Lite issues.

Tree search for LM agents: Koh et al., arXiv 2407.01476 v4, TMLR 09/2025. VisualWebArena: GPT-4o + best-first search gives "39.7% relative increase" (to 26.4% success); WebArena "28.0% relative improvement". Needs environment backtracking (not always possible, esp. with irreversible side effects); search budget increases success with diminishing returns (c, depth d, branch b ablations).

Repeated sampling: Large Language Monkeys, arXiv 2407.21787. DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct on SWE-bench Lite 15.9% (1 sample) to 56% (250 samples) vs 43% single-sample SOTA; but this is coverage with a perfect verifier (unit tests). For MATH, majority vote / reward models stay at 40.5% to 41.4% while coverage goes 82.9% to 98.4% (10,000 samples). Message: gains depend entirely on a reliable verifier. Venue not stated in the file.

Trajectory resampling / SWE-Replay (Ding and Zhang 2025) is only cited in other papers (2608.08950); not read.

VERDICT: CONFIRMED-WITH-SCOPE-CAVEAT: measured gains are real but modest after compute matching, costs multiply (about 14x for GPT-4o), all with 2024-era models; no 2026-frontier-model tree-search result read.

## 7. Ralph loop / fresh-context loops

Search: WebSearch for Ralph / fresh vs continued context / restart ablations, then read the hits.

- LoopsBench (Microsoft et al.), arXiv 2608.00267 v2, 10 Aug 2026, preprint. 112 tasks, 8 languages. Table 4: Ralph loop 13.24 context-budget rounds per run, 0.17 regressions per run, Resolve Rate 7.84%; Claude Code goal mode 34.69 rounds, 0.13, 17.65%; Codex goal mode 32.76, 0.34, 20.59%; Claude dynamic workflows 97.96, 0.36, 24.11%. Appendix N.3: "The four runs use their native model and loop configurations... not a model controlled causal comparison" (run ids: Codex GPT-5.5, Claude Code Opus 4.6 goal, Ralph with Opus 4.7, dynamic with Opus 4.8). Ralph runs have 102 scheduled tasks vs 112 for dynamic workflows. So NOT a controlled fresh-vs-continued evaluation. Also Table 2 includes "outer continuation" (an outer loop that restarts with a fresh residual): without outer loop 16.96% vs with 25.00% for Opus 4.7 + Claude Code, i.e. restarts with a freshly derived residual helped (+8.0 pts), but that is vendor loop vs outer restart, not fresh vs same-session context.
- Stateless Language Agents (SLA), arXiv 2610.07625 v1, 6 Oct 2026, preprint (Stanford, SambaNova). Harness owns state, every agent invocation gets a "fresh and role-specific context". Closest controlled evidence: Table 4 ablations from shared checkpoints (3 continuations each, 100M extra tokens): removing Advisor context reconstruction or Worker isolation (agents see the full chronological record) lowers progress in all four comparisons (e.g. FrontierSWE libexpat 16.12 checkpoint to 19.07 vs 18.78 for full, kernel 1198.3 vs 1192.3 cycles). Full 1B-token run without Worker isolation: 1336 cycles vs 1112.0 for full SLA. Also reports a CORAL run where after its last improvement at 231M tokens, 98% of the final 1,500 sessions made no tool calls while spending continued, and restarts that resume the agent's own session reproduce the stalled state. Caveat: this isolates "focused rebuilt context vs full history" inside one system (Codex, GPT-5.5), not a Ralph-style restart, and the effects after 100M tokens are small.
- LongHorizon-Harness, arXiv 2608.01964 v1, 3 Aug 2026, preprint (Alibaba). Manager + fresh-context executor + read-only auditor; Qwen 3.7-Plus Terminal-Bench 2.1 69.7% to 77.2%, WeaveBench 51.8% to 80.7%, Opus 4.7 OSWorld 2.0 subset 20.0% to 34.3%. Bundles three changes (state manager, fresh executor, auditor); no ablation isolating fresh context found by text search.
- AutoCompact, arXiv 2610.02163 v1, 1 Oct 2026, preprint. Trained compaction improves SWE-bench Verified pass rate +9.2 pts (to 39.6%) and SWE-PolyBench +5.0 pts over base; argues full history is stale; does not test fresh restart vs continue.
- SWE-Marathon (2606.07682): "Compaction tracks failure rather than rescue: 0 of 71 reward-bearing terminus-2 summarizer trials pass, against 8.9% without."
- Illusion of Diminishing Returns, arXiv 2509.09677 v3 (ICLR 2026): per-step accuracy falls as steps accumulate, and "models become more likely to make mistakes when the context contains their errors from prior turns" (self-conditioning); thinking mitigates it. Controlled, synthetic execution tasks (not coding). This is the most direct peer-reviewed mechanism argument for context resets.

VERDICT: no paper with a controlled "Ralph loop (fresh process per iteration) vs same-session continuation" comparison on equal model and task set. Searched 3 queries plus reads of 7 candidates. Better statement than "none found": "mechanism evidence for fresh/rebuilt context (self-conditioning, SLA ablations) exists; no controlled Ralph-vs-continued eval; LoopsBench's Ralph row is uncontrolled and low (7.84%)". Huntley's own posts are blog posts (ghuntley.com unreached).

## 8. Durable execution for agents

- DBOS: arXiv 2007.11112 "DBOS: A Proposal for a Data-Centric Operating System" v1, 21 Jul 2020 (no venue in the file; [MEMORY] CIDR 2021). That is an OS proposal, not the DBOS Transact durable-execution library. The Transact line: CIDR 2026 "Consistency and Correctness in Data-Oriented Workflow Systems" (Stonebraker, Zhou, Kraft, Li) [SNIPPET: venue and author block from search result; PDF at vldb.org UNREACHABLE]: argues for AC/DC workflows, durable execution with physical backout and saga compensation; transactional workflows win at low contention, sagas under contention or long steps. No arXiv version found. No SIGMOD/VLDB paper found.
- Temporal and Restate design docs: UNREACHABLE. What a read paper says about them: LogAct (Meta, arXiv 2604.07988 v1, 9 Apr 2026, cs.DC; preprint): "Systems such as Temporal and Restate operate over external, durable environments. They aim for fault-tolerant exactly-once execution by recording the results of non-deterministic operations and replaying them deterministically on recovery. However, they require the developer to define the workflow's control flow graph up-front... Agents violate both assumptions: the control flow is open-ended, with each next step determined at runtime by LLM inference over the entire history; and actions are arbitrary lambdas for which no predefined undo exists." Same paper says checkpoint/restore assumes determinism, and that agents "act upon an external, durable environment".
- Do LLM calls fit replay models? The replay-by-recorded-result model does handle non-determinism if each LLM call is a recorded step (Temporal Activity [MEMORY, docs unreachable]): on replay the stored completion is returned instead of re-calling the model, so the LLM call itself fits. What fits poorly, per LogAct, is open-ended control flow and side effects without compensation. This matches the existing 04 doc "unless every LLM call is a recorded activity", which is therefore reasoning plus secondary evidence, not primary docs.
- Atomix (arXiv 2602.14849 v2, 29 May 2026, preprint): replay/checkpoint approaches do not settle external side effects; under injected faults on WebArena/OSWorld/tau-bench workloads "every non-Checkpoint-Replay baseline falls to 0-7%" while transactional and checkpoint-replay reach 37-57%; Checkpoint-Replay is statistically tied with the full design on clean recovery (53%, 16/30 vs 17/30) but does not isolate contention or irreversible sends. Supports the plan's idempotent-effects ledger.

## 9. Long-horizon coding evals

- SWE-Marathon, arXiv 2606.07682 v1, 5 Jun 2026, preprint. 20 tasks, attempts average 27.2M tokens; no configuration exceeds 30% pass@1 (Claude Code Opus 4.8/4.7, Codex GPT-5.5, Gemini CLI, Kimi, Terminus 2 variants). Section 5.2: "Long trials contain extended runs of identical consecutive tool calls, with double-digit run lengths on most scaffolds and 877 in a row on one terminus-2 trial. Pass rate decreases monotonically with run length on three of the five primary scaffolds (claude-code 41.9% to 3.2%; kimi-cli 10.3% to 0%; gemini-cli 10.7% to 0%)." (Correlational; "run length" bins not defined in text I read.) 32% of Terminus-2's tool calls repeat an earlier (function, arguments) pair; claude-code 4%; strict waste 6-18% of the tool budget. More tokens do not help: lowest-token quintile passes 11.3%, highest 8.3%. Timeout is 31.4% of agent-attributable failures. Reward hacking in 13.8% of rollouts. Cost: a full n=5 run costs hundreds of dollars per task family; sweeps tens of thousands; token use by (model, scaffold): Opus 4.7 4.4M under Terminus 2 vs 21.9M under Claude Code.
- Terminal-Bench 2.0, arXiv 2601.11868, ICLR 2026. 89 tasks; top models under 65%. "Most agents attempt tasks for less than 20 minutes. However, in some cases, they take up to two hours, making hundreds of API calls and using almost 100 million tokens on a single task"; "essentially no correlation between the number of average turns per trial and model success rates"; run cost 1 to 100 dollars. Error analysis derived from MAST.
- SlopCodeBench, arXiv 2603.24755 v2 (7 May 2026), preprint: 36 problems, 196 checkpoints, 15 agents; best passes 14.8% of checkpoints; structural erosion rises in 77% of trajectories and verbosity in 75.5% (earlier version/snippet said 80% / 89.8%: quote v2 only); cost per checkpoint +12.1% (v2). Degradation across iterations, not within one run.
- LoopsBench (section 7): best configuration 25.00% resolved; regression events visible in every loop profile (0.13-0.36 per run).
- Illusion of Diminishing Returns (ICLR 2026): per-step accuracy degradation and self-conditioning on own errors.
- METR (section 2): success falls exponentially with human task length; stronger models repeat failed actions less (2/32 for o1 vs 12/31 for GPT-4 1106) and abandon prematurely more.
- Runaway cost / loops in unattended runs: the papers above give quantitative evidence for repetition and 100M-token tails (Terminal-Bench, SWE-Marathon). Specific dollar anecdotes found only in vendor blogs [SNIPPET], not used.

## 10. Builder self-check vs independent verifier

No paper found that runs the same coding agent as builder-self-verifier vs a separate verifier agent with matched model strength and reports resolve rates. Indirect numbers:
- Valmeekam et al., "Can LLMs Really Improve by Self-critiquing Their Own Plans?" arXiv 2310.08118 v1 (Oct 2023, preprint), GPT-4, Blocksworld, 100 instances: no backprompting 40%; LLM generator + LLM verifier backprompting 55% (3.48 iterations); generator + sound external verifier (VAL) 88% (4.18 iterations). The LLM verifier was 61% accurate, with 7 of 45 incorrect plans passed (false positives; Table 2). The abstract says self-critique "appears to diminish" performance relative to the external verifier. Note the same paper's Table 1 shows the self-critique loop above no-backprompt, so quote 55 vs 88.
- Stechly et al., arXiv 2310.12397 (graph colouring, GPT-4): LLM self-critique no better than generation; external verifier gains largely come from top-k luck. Not read in detail beyond abstract.
- Kamoi et al., arXiv 2406.01297 v3: critical survey; no consensus, negative results for self-feedback (abstract only read).
- RETRACE, arXiv 2608.08950 v1 (9 Aug 2026, preprint): SWE-bench Verified, mini-SWE-agent. GPT-5-mini 56.2% baseline, Self-Refine (same-model review of patch) 54.8% (-1.4), RETRACE (independent backward reconstruction of the problem from the patch, same backbone) 63.2% (+7.0); MiniMax M2.5 75.8%, Self-Refine 74.0% (-1.8), RETRACE 79.4% (+3.6). Caveat: on OpenHands (120-issue subset) Self-Refine helped (37.5 to 53.3 GPT-5-mini; 62.5 to 64.2 MiniMax), RETRACE 56.7 / 70.0, so same-model self-review is scaffold-dependent. The verifier is the same model family, so this tests independence of information, not of model.
- SWE Refactor Bench, arXiv 2608.23564 (Aug 2026, preprint): of 88 submissions that passed every fixed check, six independent agentic verifiers (one hour each) broke 60 (68.2%) with an executable counterexample; only 28 survived all six; the average submission survived 3.94 of 6. "A strong coding agent as the verifier matters an order of magnitude more than its configuration." Shows fixed tests are insufficient, not builder vs verifier.
- SWE-Marathon Table 4 / Section 5: poor self-verification is the primary label for 4.0% of attributable failures (21 of 526) but the text says weak local validation is a wider contributor; a worked case has the agent's own test loop reporting "34212 passed, failed=0" while the hidden suite failed. Also reward hacking in 13.8% of rollouts.
- LongHorizon-Harness: read-only auditor with fresh context as the sole source of "done"; gains bundled (section 7).
- The Verification Horizon (Qwen team, arXiv 2606.26300 v2, 29 Jun 2026): argues every verifier is a proxy and must co-evolve with the generator; no builder vs verifier comparison extracted.

VERDICT: NOT-IN-PAPER for a direct matched comparison. Indirect evidence consistently supports "ground in executed checks; do not let the builder's own review flip criteria", and says an LLM verifier alone is also unreliable (61% accurate in Valmeekam). That matches the plan's "hybrid verification" default.
